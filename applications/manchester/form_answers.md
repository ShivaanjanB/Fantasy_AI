**Research summary (if a short box appears, ~150 words):**
> In October 2024 the UK introduced mandatory reimbursement for authorised push payment fraud, splitting liability equally between sending and receiving payment providers. This project treats the change as a natural experiment. Study 1 uses Payment Systems Regulator firm-level data in a difference-in-differences design to test whether firms more exposed to the new liability changed their fraud outcomes. Study 2 builds a cost-aligned benchmark for receiving-side mule-account detection under delayed labels and concept drift, comparing gradient-boosted, account-level and graph neural network models and measuring how much accuracy explainability costs. Study 3 uses interviews with UK banks and fintechs to explain how firms reorganised their fraud detection, data sharing and model governance. The project links incentive theory with information systems research on payment infrastructures and offers evidence for regulators and industry.
