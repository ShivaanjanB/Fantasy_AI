**Subject:** Prospective PhD applicant (2027 entry): liability shifts and mule-account detection in UK instant payments

Dear Professor Zachariadis,

I am an MSc Financial Technology student at the University of Warwick and I plan to apply for a PhD at AMBS for September 2027 entry, with the AMBS School Studentship (deadline 1 December). I am writing to ask whether you might be willing to supervise the project outlined below.

Since October 2024, the PSR's mandatory reimbursement regime for APP fraud has split liability equally between sending and receiving PSPs. In effect, it shifts part of the cost of fraud onto the institutions that host mule accounts. My proposed project treats this as a natural experiment. It would test whether firms more exposed to the change saw different fraud outcomes, using PSR firm-level data in a difference-in-differences design. It would benchmark receiving-side mule detection under delayed labels and the regime's real cost structure, using graph-based and explainable models. And it would examine, through interviews, how banks and fintechs reorganised their detection work.

Your work on payment infrastructures and the governance of financial technology, particularly *[name a specific paper you've read and one sentence on how it relates]*, is the main reason I am applying to AMBS.
