## Which programs are realistic vs reach for this profile?

### Takeaway
Nearly every program that clears the ~CAD 31k threshold expects a GMAT/GRE score. Several require it and will not waive it (Ivey, Alberta, DeGroote, SFU, and in practice Queen's for a non-Canadian degree). Without a strong test score, most of this list is out of reach for this applicant. McGill (GMAT optional) is the main place where the lack of a test does not by itself rule him out. With a score at or above the 80th–90th percentile, the realistic band is Haskayne, Alberta, Waterloo Accounting, Telfer, Queen's Smith and Concordia. UBC Sauder, Rotman, Ivey and McGill are reaches.

### Cited Findings
- UBC Sauder: "no official cut-off" for GMAT/GRE, but "the average score of successful applicants is about the 95th percentile." — [UBC Sauder How to apply](https://www.sauder.ubc.ca/programs/phd/phd-business-administration/admission-finance/how-to-apply)
- McGill Desautels: GMAT is "optional but recommended". Admits over the past five years averaged a GMAT of 682. — [McGill Desautels PhD FAQ](https://www.mcgill.ca/desautels/programs/phd/admissions/faqs)
- Ivey: a GRE/GMAT/GMAT Focus score "may not be waived or substituted". Ivey "strongly prefers" verbal and quant each at or above the 75th percentile. — [Ivey Apply](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/phd/admissions/apply/)
- Haskayne: recommended GMAT is 650 (10th ed.) or 595 (Focus), or GRE at or above the 70th percentile. Haskayne admits 8–10 students a year. — [Haskayne PhD admissions](https://haskayne.ucalgary.ca/future-students/doctoral-programs/phd/admissions); [F2026 PhD Program Information PDF](https://haskayne.ucalgary.ca/sites/default/files/teams/9/F2026%20PhD%20Program%20Information.doc.pdf) **[EARLIER CYCLE]**
- Queen's Smith: a GRE or GMAT score is required. Only applicants with a degree from a Canadian institution can request an exemption. — [Smith PhD Admission Process](https://smith.queensu.ca/grad_studies/PHD/program-details/admissions.php)
- Alberta: all applicants must submit GMAT or GRE scores. — [Alberta School of Business PhD Admissions](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/business/programs/phd/admission-requirements/index.html)
- McMaster DeGroote: GMAT/GRE scores are required and are valid for 5 years. — [DeGroote PhD Admissions](https://phd.degroote.mcmaster.ca/program-details/admissions/)
- SFU Beedie: a valid GMAT or GRE score is required. — [SFU Beedie PhD Admissions](https://www.sfu.ca/beedie/programs/phd-business-administration/admissions.html)

### Inferences
- **Applicant's strengths:** a first-class undergraduate record (CGPA 9.475/10, topped the final year), a quantitative MSc FinTech from Warwick, and hands-on ML and fintech projects. **Weaknesses:** the undergraduate institution is not widely known to Canadian committees, the papers are not in top-tier venues, and there is no GMAT/GRE. The missing test is the binding constraint. Almost every program below requires one.
- **Reach:** UBC Sauder (95th-percentile average), Rotman, Ivey (75th percentile or higher on both sections), McGill (average GMAT 682, a very small cohort).
- **Competitive but plausible with a strong GRE/GMAT:** Queen's Smith, Alberta, Haskayne, Waterloo Accounting (if accounting research appeals), Telfer, Concordia JMSB, HEC Montréal.
- **More attainable, but funding falls below the threshold:** Schulich, DeGroote, Concordia and HEC (in stipend terms).
- **Fit:** Information Systems / MIS tracks (Sauder, McGill, Queen's, Alberta, Haskayne, Concordia, HEC, Ivey) fit his blockchain-payments and ML background at least as well as Finance does. Finance tracks tend to be the most test-score driven.
- **Timing:** Queen's (Oct 31) and Haskayne (Dec 1) close first. A GRE sitting in October 2026 is needed to make those deadlines.

### Gaps
- None of the admission pages gave admit rates by country of origin or by area.
- The English-test minimums found cover Concordia only. The ones for Sauder, Rotman, Ivey and the rest were not confirmed. A UK MSc may qualify for a waiver at some schools; this was not verified.
- Whether GMAT/GRE is required at Rotman, Waterloo, Telfer and HEC Montréal was not confirmed.

Programs that clearly meet the ≥ CAD 31k threshold **and** cover international tuition: **UBC Sauder** (40k), **Rotman** (40k/34.3k), **Alberta** (35k) and, if tuition is covered, **Queen's Smith** (34k). **Ivey** (37k) and **Telfer** (up to 36–47k) have higher headline figures, but students appear to pay their own tuition, so the net amount is lower. **McGill** and **HEC** give 30k with tuition covered (just under the threshold). **Haskayne** gives 27k with tuition paid, and **Waterloo** a 28.4k minimum. **Concordia, Schulich and DeGroote** fall below the threshold.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Best net funding for an international student:** UBC Sauder (CAD 40k ≈ USD 29k, tuition covered, and Vancouver is expensive), Rotman (34.3–40k, tuition covered), Alberta (35k ≈ USD 25k, tuition covered, lower cost of living), and possibly Queen's (34k).
- **Ivey's net is uncertain.** Ivey's $37k appears to be gross, with the student paying Western tuition from it. International PhD tuition at Western is not sourced here. If it is being charged at domestic rates or offset, the net could still be above 31k; this needs confirming.
- **Haskayne vs. the threshold.** Haskayne's 27k with all tuition paid is below the 31k stipend line. Calgary's lower living costs may make it roughly equivalent.
- **Montreal programs.** McGill's and HEC's 30k with tuition covered is just under the threshold, and Montreal's cost of living is lower. Quebec (CAQ) permits add paperwork.
- **Excluded on funding grounds:** Schulich (25k), DeGroote (20k) and Concordia (~20k/yr after tuition) fall short unless the student wins extra awards.
- **Application fees:** confirmed fees run CAD 130–170 (≈USD 94–121) per school. Applying to 8–10 programs costs about CAD 1,200–1,500 (my arithmetic).
